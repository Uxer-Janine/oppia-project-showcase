The project

About Oppia and my role.

Oppia is an open-source learning platform where people can work through lessons, practice skills and revisit what they’ve learned.

I explored how learners use the platform, used it in my community sessions, and worked on ways to reach learners and thank supporters.

Research & Usability

Can learners tell what to do next?

I wanted to find out whether learners could make sense of a topic page, start a lesson and choose their next step.

3participants
3layouts explored
20–21 Oct 2024in-person study sessions

What I looked at

I tested three layouts: a horizontal layout, a vertical redesign and the website layout in use at the time. I looked at how learners understood the lesson and practice controls, followed their progress, and started, resumed or reviewed lessons.

Each participant tried one layout on a laptop. I recorded what they did and said, asked follow-up questions, and gathered their feedback through a questionnaire.

What I wanted to understand

Does the page help learners know what to do next?

Explore → Start → Check progress → Continue or review
  1. Explore

    I watched what learners noticed and explored first.

  2. Try the tasks

    I asked them to start, switch, continue and review lessons.

  3. Ask what makes sense

    I asked what the icons, filters and progress cues meant to them.

  4. Bring the findings together

    I compared my notes with their questionnaire responses.

What I found

Meaning

The icons weren’t always clear.

Learners could explain the difference between lessons and practice, but the book and pencil icons didn’t always mean what they expected.

My recommendation: add clear labels beside the icons and explain what learners can do in each activity.

Discovery

Learners sometimes missed the filters.

Some learners overlooked the lesson and practice checkboxes or weren’t sure what they did, even after I asked them to try them.

My recommendation: explain what the filters do and make it easy to see what changes when one is selected.

Orientation

Learners sometimes needed help seeing their progress.

Some learners weren’t sure which lesson they were on or needed a prompt to notice their progress. The start and review actions were often easier to find.

My recommendation: keep the familiar start and review actions, and make it clearer which lesson is current, completed or next.

These are my recommendations based on the findings. With one participant per layout, I could identify usability issues, but couldn’t reliably choose a winning design.

I wrote the research report on 22 October 2024. I’ve shared the findings and interface images here without including participant records.

What I did

I started by teaching technology skills. When parents asked about mathematics, science and languages, I expanded the sessions. Oppia helped me work with children across grade levels, on weekends during the school term and during holidays.

We covered place values, addition, subtraction, multiplication and division, as well as financial literacy and some science topics. In my account of the sessions, I also noted plans to cover decimals, ratios and percentages.

What I noticed

  • I noticed learners becoming more confident and enthusiastic.
  • Children invited friends to join the sessions.
  • Parents got involved in practice at home.

These are my observations from the sessions, rather than measured learning gains.

Engagement Strategy · SEO & Data Analysis

Helping more people discover and use Oppia.

I audited the website and looked at how other learning platforms engage people, then suggested ways Oppia could reach more learners and encourage them to keep learning.

Looking at the website

I used SEOptimer to review the homepage’s SEO, links, usability, performance and social presence. I documented keyword rankings, estimated search traffic and backlinks, and organized the recommendations by priority.

The audit flagged a meta description as a high priority, along with other work on titles, headings and canonical tags. It reflects what the tool reported at the time, rather than the website as it is today.

Ideas for reaching and engaging learners

I looked at approaches used by Coursera and Khan Academy and suggested ideas for Oppia: accessible mobile and offline learning, locally relevant content, educator involvement, clearer reasons to keep learning, and content built around relevant search terms.

My audit and engagement analysis explain the thinking behind these proposals.

Outcomes & Reflections

What came out of the work.

I brought together learner research, hands-on tutoring, website analysis and communication design.

Usability research

I produced a usability report covering three layouts, with observations and feedback that pointed to clearer icons, filters and progress cues.

Community learning

I used Oppia in my tutoring sessions and wrote about what we covered, how children participated and what I noticed along the way.

Analysis and communication

I created a homepage SEO audit, an engagement analysis, a donor-recognition proposal and original communication materials.

What to explore next

The work raises a few practical questions: how can a page help learners without someone explaining it? What can research and website data tell us together? And how can communication make Oppia’s purpose clear to learners, parents and supporters?

The next steps would be to test the navigation ideas with more learners, check the audit findings against the current site, and measure any strategy that is adopted. I’m sharing my recommendations as proposals; the evidence here doesn’t show that they were implemented or led to gains in learning, traffic or donations.
