Research & Usability

Helping learners identify their next step.

Can learners understand a topic page, start a lesson and recognize where to go next?

3participants
3layouts explored
20–21 Oct 2024in-person study sessions

What I investigated

I evaluated a horizontal layout, a vertical redesign and the then-current website layout. The study examined the meaning of lesson and practice controls, how learners recognized progress, and how they started, resumed and reviewed lessons.

Each participant explored one layout on a laptop. I documented their actions, answers and responses to follow-up prompts, then collected questionnaire feedback.

The design question

Does the page make the next learning action clear?

Explore → Start → Recognize progress → Resume or review
  1. Explore

    Observe first impressions and which controls attract attention.

  2. Follow tasks

    Ask learners to start, switch, continue and review lessons.

  3. Probe meaning

    Ask what icons, filters and progress indicators communicate.

  4. Synthesize

    Compare recorded observations and questionnaire responses.

What the study revealed

Meaning

Icons did not always explain the activity.

Learners could distinguish learning from practice in conversation, but the book and pencil controls could still be interpreted differently.

Recommended direction: pair icons with plain-language labels and explain what each activity involves.

Discovery

Filters were easy to overlook.

The lesson and practice checkboxes were sometimes unnoticed or misunderstood, including after prompting.

Recommended direction: make filter purpose and the effect of a selection visible.

Orientation

Progress cues needed a clearer connection to the next action.

Some learners were unsure which lesson they were on or needed a prompt to notice progress indicators. Start and review actions were often discoverable.

Recommended direction: retain recognizable start/review actions and make current, completed and next states more explicit.

Recommendations above synthesize my findings. They are proposed directions, not a record of shipped changes. With one participant per layout, the study does not establish a statistically reliable winning design.

Community Engagement

Taking learning beyond the interface.

My community work brought Oppia into in-person tutoring and virtual learning sessions.

What I did

I began with technology skills and broadened the sessions as parents expressed interest in mathematics, science and languages. I used Oppia to work with learners across grade levels, with sessions on weekends during the school term and during holidays.

My learning-journey account documents foundational mathematics, including place values, addition, subtraction, multiplication and division, alongside financial literacy and some science topics. Decimals, ratios and percentages were recorded as future plans.

Observed in my sessions

  • Learners showed greater confidence and enthusiasm.
  • Children invited friends to join the sessions.
  • Parents became involved in practice at home.

These are qualitative observations recorded in my account, rather than measured learning gains or an evaluation of causal impact.

Engagement Strategy · SEO & Data Analysis

Understanding how people find and engage with Oppia.

I combined a website audit with comparative engagement analysis to identify opportunities for discovery and continued learning.

Audit and analysis

My homepage SEO audit used SEOptimer to examine on-page SEO, links, usability, performance and social signals. It documented keyword rankings, estimated search traffic and backlink information, alongside a prioritized recommendation list.

The report placed a meta description among high-priority recommendations and identified title, heading and canonical-tag work among other opportunities. These are historical tool-reported findings, not a statement about Oppia’s current website.

From analysis to proposed strategy

I examined engagement approaches used by Coursera and Khan Academy, then proposed tactics for Oppia: accessible mobile and offline learning, localized content and educator involvement, clearer learning incentives, and keyword-focused content.

These documents demonstrate my analytical and strategy work. They do not establish that the recommendations were implemented or that traffic, retention or conversions improved.

Outcomes & Reflections

What this work contributes.

A documented view of learner needs, practical community activity, and materials for discovery and supporter engagement.

Research evidence

An authored usability report with task observations and questionnaire responses across three layouts, informing clear opportunities around meaning, discovery and orientation.

Community practice

In-person and virtual tutoring using Oppia, with a written account of covered subjects, participation and qualitative observations.

Analysis and communication

A homepage SEO audit, comparative engagement analysis, a donor-recognition proposal and original visual communication materials.

Questions the work brings forward

How can lesson navigation explain itself before a learner needs help? How can research observations and analytics complement each other? And how can communication make the purpose of a learning platform clear to learners, parents and supporters?

Further evaluation could test the navigation recommendations with more learners, check audit findings against the current site, and measure any adopted engagement strategy against a defined baseline. Those are next steps, not results demonstrated here.
